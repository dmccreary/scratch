    Abstraction Layers Explorer¶

    Run the Abstraction Layers Explorer MicroSim Fullscreen
    Edit in the p5.js Editor

    About This MicroSim¶

    Abstraction means giving a group of steps one short name, and then using the name instead of the steps. The important part is that the steps do not go away. The name only hides them.

    This MicroSim lets you open up one name and look underneath:

    • What you say is one purple block: draw flower.
    • What that means is three blue blocks.
    • What the computer does is eight green blocks.

    Each layer has more to remember than the one above it. That is why a good name is so useful: you only have to hold one thing in your head.

    How to Use¶

    1. Start at Detail level 1. You see one name. That is all you have to remember.
    2. Slide Detail level to 2. A new band slides open to show what the name was really made of.
    3. Slide to 3 to see the steps the computer follows.
    4. Turn on Show step count to see how many things you would have to remember at each layer: 1, then 3, then 8.
    5. Slide back to 1. The detail hides again, but it is still there.
    6. Press Reset to go back to the start.

    On a narrow screen the eight green blocks wrap onto two rows.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Grade Level¶

    Grades 3-5 (elementary), beginning computational thinking with Scratch

    Duration¶

    10-15 minutes

    Learning Objective¶

    The student can explain that naming a group of steps hides detail without removing it, and can state how many underlying steps a single named block stands for. (Bloom's Taxonomy: Understand)

    Prerequisites¶

    • Decomposition: a big job is made of smaller jobs
    • The "Draw Square" worked example in Chapter 1

    Activities¶

    1. Predict (3 min): With the slider at 1, ask: "How many steps do you think are hiding inside draw flower?" Write the guesses on the board.
    2. Open It Up (5 min): Students slide to 2 and then 3 with Show step count turned on. They read each caption out loud.
    3. Hide It Again (2 min): Students slide back to 1. Ask: "Did the eight steps disappear? Where did they go?" (They are still there. The name hides them.)
    4. Name Your Own (5 min): Students pick an everyday job, such as "set the table," and write the name on one line and the hidden steps underneath it.

    Teacher Note¶

    The eight green blocks are the steps that draw one square. A real flower program repeats those steps eight times, so the full list is even longer than the bottom band shows. That makes a good extension question: "If one square is eight steps, how many steps is the whole flower?"

    Assessment¶

    • Can the student say in their own words what a name hides?
    • Can the student say how many steps one named block stands for at each layer?
    • Can the student explain why Student B's short description in the chapter is not missing anything?
