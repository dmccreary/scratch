    Decomposing a House Drawing¶

    Run the Decomposing a House Drawing MicroSim Fullscreen

    About This MicroSim¶

    Decomposition means breaking a big job into smaller jobs, and breaking those into even smaller ones, until every piece is small enough to hand to a computer. This MicroSim shows that for one job: draw a house.

    The tree has four levels. The top level is the whole job. Each level below it is more detailed and less fuzzy than the one above:

    • Purple is the whole job.
    • Blue and orange boxes are still too big to program.
    • Green boxes, marked with a check, are small enough to be one Scratch block.

    The change from orange to green is the message of the whole diagram: green means "small enough to program."

    How to Use¶

    1. Rest the mouse on any box. A small tip answers the question "Small enough to program?" with Yes or Not yet.
    2. Click a box. The panel at the bottom gives the answer and the reason.
    3. Look at the thick dark line. It shows the path from the box you clicked back up to "Draw a house," so you can see which big job the small step belongs to. The other boxes fade.
    4. Click the empty background to clear the highlight.

    Try to predict the answer before you click. Then check.

    The boxes cannot be dragged and the picture cannot be zoomed, so the layout always stays the same.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Grade Level¶

    Grades 3-5 (elementary), beginning computational thinking with Scratch

    Duration¶

    10-15 minutes

    Learning Objective¶

    Given a drawing task, the student can identify which parts of the task are still too big to program and which parts are small enough to become a single instruction. (Bloom's Taxonomy: Analyze)

    Prerequisites¶

    • The idea that a program is a list of steps done in order
    • The worked example "Drawing a House" in Chapter 1

    Activities¶

    1. Predict (3 min): Before clicking anything, students point to each box and say "Yes" or "Not yet." They click to check.
    2. Trace a Path (5 min): Students click "Move forward 100 steps" under the walls and read the path line out loud, from the top job down to the block. They repeat for one green box under the roof.
    3. Spot the Difference (3 min): Students compare the two "Turn right" boxes. Why is one 90 degrees and the other 120 degrees? (A square has four corners and a triangle has three, and the turns always add up to one full circle of 360 degrees.)
    4. Your Turn (5 min): On paper, students draw their own tree for a new job, such as "draw a kite" or "draw a robot face," and color the boxes that are small enough to be one block.

    Assessment¶

    • Can the student say why "Draw one side" is not yet small enough?
    • Can the student name all the single-block steps needed for the walls?
    • Given a new drawing job, can the student keep cutting until every piece is one instruction?

    References¶

    1. Decomposition (computer science) - Wikipedia - Breaking a complex problem into parts that are easier to understand and program.
    2. Turtle graphics - Wikipedia - The move-and-turn drawing style used for the house, including why the turns of a closed shape add up to 360 degrees.
    3. Scratch - MIT Media Lab / Scratch Foundation - The block-based programming language used throughout this book.
    4. vis-network Documentation - vis.js - Documentation for the JavaScript library used to draw this tree.
