## About This MicroSim

Computational thinking is made of four powers: **decomposition**, **pattern
recognition**, **abstraction** and **algorithm design**. This MicroSim puts
each power on its own colored card. Click a card and the panel beside it shows
three things:

- what the power means, in one sentence
- an example from school life
- an example from Scratch

The card colors are Scratch block colors, and the same colors are used for
these four ideas in the other MicroSims in this chapter.

## How to Use

1. Move the mouse over a card. The card lifts a little, which tells you it can
   be clicked.
2. Click the card. It gets a white border and the panel fills in.
3. Read the definition and both examples. Try to say the idea in your own words.
4. Click the other three cards. The counter at the bottom of the panel keeps
   track of how many powers you have explored.
5. Press **Start Over** to clear the panel and the counter.

On a narrow screen the four cards stack on top of each other and the panel
moves below them.

## Lesson Plan

### Grade Level
Grades 3-5 (elementary), beginning computational thinking with Scratch

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Learning Objective
Given the name of one of the four powers of computational thinking, the
student can explain in their own words what that power means and give one
everyday example of it. (Bloom's Taxonomy: Understand)

### Prerequisites
None. This is the first MicroSim in the book. Students only need to be able to
click with a mouse or tap on a tablet.

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Students click all four cards and read each panel.
   They stop when the panel says "You have explored all four powers!"
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): The teacher names one power. A student explains
   it without looking, then clicks the card to check. Repeat for all four.
3. **Make It Yours** (5 min): In pairs, students invent one new everyday
   example for each power (for example, decomposition: "packing for a trip is
   really packing clothes, packing a toothbrush, and packing a book").

### Assessment
- Can the student match each of the four names to its meaning?
- Can the student give an example that is not one of the examples in the panel?
- Can the student say which power comes first in this chapter, and why the
  others lean on it?
